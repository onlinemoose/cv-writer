# Method

How to build the CV. A caller can replace this whole file with their own
method; when they do, the standards and the output format still hold.

## Read the posting first

Identify the three to six things that decide the shortlist for this role:
the core skills, the level of responsibility, the domain, and any hard
requirement such as a named tool, a certification, or a clearance.
Everything below serves putting the candidate's evidence for those things
where it is read first.

## Order the document to the posting

Default order: identity block, professional summary, key skills,
professional experience, education, then anything else (certifications,
publications, languages). Move a section up when this posting makes it
decisive — a certifications block near the top for a role that names one,
a projects section above employment where the projects are the strongest
evidence. Put the strongest match for this posting on the first page.

## Professional summary

Two to four lines, specific to this posting. Lead with the thread through
the candidate's career that makes this role the next step — take it from
the emphasis points if any are given, otherwise read the CV against the
posting and name it. State years of experience, the domains worked in,
and the level reached, drawn from the sources. The summary is evidence,
not adjectives.

## Key skills

A short grouped list drawn from the sources, ordered to the posting's
priorities. Use the posting's own term for a skill where the sources
support the same thing under another name. List a skill only where the
experience section evidences it.

## Professional experience

Reverse-chronological. Each entry is one line of job title, employer,
location, and dates, then achievement lines. Give the roles and lines
that serve this posting the most room — four to six lines for a directly
relevant recent role, one or two for an old or unrelated one.

Write each line as an achievement: a strong past-tense verb, what was
done, and the outcome. Where the sources give a figure for scope, scale,
or result, use it; where they give none, state the outcome plainly
without one. Within the grounding rules, reframe a source line to
foreground what this posting cares about without changing what it claims.

Where the candidate has a career gap, a short stint, or a personal
venture, present the experience for what it is and let the surrounding
lines carry its relevance. Keep any explanation the sources give.

## Education and the rest

Reverse-chronological, trimmed to what a reader of this posting needs
plus what the region expects. Keep degree, institution, and year; keep or
drop detail such as modules or grades by relevance and region norm.

## Length

Aim for the region's norm — up to two pages for a UK CV, one page for a
US resume — unless a target length says otherwise. Reach it by adjusting
the detail under roles, cutting the least relevant first.

## Voice

Keep the candidate's own wording where it already reads well; tighten
where it is verbose. The finished CV should sound like the candidate,
edited.
