- Read the job posting first and name the few things that most decide
  the shortlist — usually three to six: the core skills, the level of
  responsibility, the domain, and any hard requirement (a certification,
  a clearance, a named tool). Everything below is ordered to put the
  candidate's evidence for those things where it is read first.

- Order the document so the strongest match for this posting is on the
  first page. The usual order is: identity block, professional summary,
  key skills, professional experience, education, then anything else
  (certifications, publications, languages). Move a section up when this
  posting makes it decisive — a certifications block near the top for a
  role that names the certification, a projects section above employment
  for a role where the projects are the strongest evidence.

- Professional summary: two to four lines, tailored to this posting.
  Lead with the thread running through the candidate's career that makes
  this role the next step — take it from the emphasis points if any are
  given, otherwise read the CV against the posting and name it. State
  years of experience, the domains worked in, and the level reached, all
  drawn from the source. No adjectives of self-assessment ("driven",
  "passionate", "results-oriented").

- Key skills: a short grouped list drawn from the source, ordered to
  match the posting's priorities and using the posting's own terms where
  the source supports the same skill under a different name. Include a
  skill only where the experience section evidences it. Do not pad the
  list to look comprehensive.

- Professional experience: reverse-chronological. Each entry is one line
  of job title, employer, location, and dates, then achievement bullets.
  Give the roles and bullets that serve this posting the most space —
  four to six bullets for a directly relevant recent role, one or two
  for an old or unrelated one. Keep every role's header line.

- Write each bullet as an achievement, not a duty: a strong past-tense
  verb, what was done, and the outcome. Use the figures the source
  supplies for scope, scale, or result; where it gives none, state the
  outcome plainly without a number. Never invent or round up a figure.
  Reframe a source bullet to foreground the part this posting cares
  about, but do not change what it claims.

- Education and the rest: reverse-chronological, trimmed to what a
  reader of this posting needs plus what the region expects. Keep
  degree, institution, and year. Drop or keep detail (modules, grades)
  by relevance and region norm.

- Do not create a section the source has no material for. If the posting
  wants something the candidate has no evidence of, leave it out of the
  CV and record it as a gap in the note.

- Length: aim for the region's norm — up to two pages for a UK CV, one
  page for a US resume — unless a target length says otherwise. Reach it
  by adjusting the detail under roles, cutting the least relevant first.

- Keep the candidate's own wording where it already reads well; tighten
  where it is verbose. The finished CV should sound like the candidate,
  edited — not like a different writer.
